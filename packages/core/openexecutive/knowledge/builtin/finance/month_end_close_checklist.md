# Month-End Close Checklist for a Small Company

Closing a month means making last month's books complete and correct, then stopping anyone from changing them. In a small company the close is rarely slow because the work is hard. It is slow because a few items are left open that nobody sees until the end: a bank line nobody matched, a supplier bill still in draft, a delivery never invoiced. Each has an owner, but nobody is looking.

This checklist is a standard to compare a company's month against. For each item it says what to look at, what counts as done, and who usually owns it. A close is ready when every item is done or has a named owner and a date.

## How to Use It

- **Check every item, and say what was checked.** A list of blockers is only useful if the reader also sees what was looked at and found clear. A blocker that was never checked is worse than one that was reported and turned out to be fine.
- **Give each open item an owner,** from the people and roles the company has. Most items belong to whoever keeps the books (a controller or bookkeeper); some need the person who approves the spend or the customer's account.
- **Use the month's figures,** with the period, the currency and the report or document each comes from.

## 1. Bank and Cash

- **Bank accounts reconciled to the statement** at the month's end date. Every statement line is matched to an entry in the books, and every payment or receipt in the books for the month appears on the statement or is listed as outstanding (a cheque not yet cleared, a transfer in transit).
- **Unmatched lines named:** each with its date, amount and what it probably is. An unmatched receipt is often a customer payment not yet allocated to its invoice; an unmatched charge is often a bank fee or a direct debit not yet booked.
- **Cash and petty cash counted** where the company keeps them.

*Usually owned by:* whoever keeps the books reconciles; whoever approved a payment answers for an unknown one.

## 2. Sales Complete

- **Everything delivered last month is invoiced.** List goods delivered or services completed in the month with no customer invoice. Each one is income missing from the month.
- **Customer payments allocated** to the invoices they pay, so the receivables list is right.
- **Credit notes and returns** for the month entered.

*Usually owned by:* whoever invoices customers; the account's owner where a delivery is disputed.

## 3. Purchases Complete

- **No supplier bill from last month left in draft.** A draft is invisible to the books: the cost and the amount owed are both missing until it is submitted.
- **Everything received last month is billed or accrued.** Goods or services received in the month with no supplier bill are a cost of the month. Either the bill is entered, or an accrual records it until the bill arrives.
- **Supplier statements compared** for the largest suppliers, so a missing bill shows up.

*Usually owned by:* whoever enters supplier bills; the person who approved the purchase where a bill is queried.

## 4. Adjustments

- **Prepayments released.** Costs paid in advance (insurance, annual subscriptions, rent paid ahead) are released to the month by the month's share. Check that this month's release was posted.
- **Accruals posted** for costs of the month not yet billed (utilities, professional fees), and last month's accruals reversed if the bills arrived.
- **Depreciation** posted for fixed assets.
- **Payroll** for the month posted, with its taxes and any amounts withheld.
- **Foreign-currency balances revalued** at the month's closing rate: receivables, payables and bank accounts held in another currency. The gain or loss on revaluation belongs to the month.
- **Recurring journals** the company posts every month (rent, interest, allocations) all present.

*Usually owned by:* whoever keeps the books; an accountant for depreciation methods, tax and anything unusual.

## 5. Review

- **Trial balance balances,** and no unexplained balance sits in a suspense or clearing account.
- **Compare to budget and to last month.** A line that moved a lot is either real (explain it) or a missing or misposted entry (fix it).
- **Receivables and payables ageing** agree with the balance-sheet totals.
- **Tax accounts** (sales tax or VAT collected and paid, payroll taxes) agree with what will be filed. Whether a filing is right is a tax adviser's or accountant's question.

## 6. Lock the Month

- **No more postings into the closed month.** Lock the period, or set the date before which nobody may post. Until it is locked, anyone with access can change last month's figures after the reports have gone out.
- **Closing entry** where the books need one at a year end, carrying profit or loss into retained earnings.
- **Reports issued** (profit and loss, balance sheet, cash, the budget comparison) with the date they were run.

*Usually owned by:* whoever keeps the books locks the period; the owner or finance lead signs off the month.

## Options When Something Is Still Open

An open item doesn't always have to hold the close. The usual choices, each decided by the person who owns that item:

| Situation | Options |
| --- | --- |
| A bank line can't be identified | Hold the close until it is; or post it to a clearing account with a note and clear it next month |
| A supplier bill hasn't arrived | Accrue the cost from the purchase order or receipt; or close without it and accept the month's cost is understated |
| A delivery wasn't invoiced | Invoice it now, dated in the month if the company's policy allows; or accrue the income |
| The month can't be locked yet | Lock it with a known list of exceptions; or leave it open with a date to lock |

Name who decides each one. Anything that changes tax, or how something is treated in the accounts, is for an accountant to confirm.

## Keeping the Close Short

- Do part of the work during the month: reconcile the bank weekly, enter bills when they arrive, invoice on delivery.
- Keep the same checklist every month, with an owner per line, and note what held the close up. The same blocker twice is a process to fix.
- Close within a fixed number of working days, and lock the period on that day.
