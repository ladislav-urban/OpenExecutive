# Customers' Credit Exposure: Limit Used, Payment Habit, What an Order Adds

A credit limit caps how much a customer may owe at once. Seeing each customer's exposure is a reading of the books: what the limit is, what is owed against it, how the customer has paid, and what a new order would take it to. Deciding whether to change a limit, release an order or refuse one is a credit decision, and belongs to a person.

## What Each Customer Owes Against Its Limit

- **The limit** comes from the customer's record, per company.
- **What it owes** is its open receivables, plus orders already confirmed but not yet invoiced if the company's credit check counts them. Say which the figure includes.
- **The share used** = what it owes ÷ its limit. Show both figures and the percentage, with the company and currency.
- **Closest to its limit first** is a sort by share used, not a ranking of customers. A customer with no limit set is listed as such, not as "unlimited".

## How Each Has Paid

- **The payment habit** comes from the payment ledger: for the invoices paid over a period, the usual days from invoice date to payment, against the terms. Give the period and the number of invoices it rests on.
- **Say what changed:** a customer that usually pays on time and is late for the first time reads differently from one that always takes two to three months. Both are facts from the records, not a judgement on the customer.
- **Open and overdue now:** what is past due today, and by how many days.

## What an Order Adds

- **The exposure with the order** = what the customer owes now + the order's value. Compare it with the limit.
- **The limit the order would need** is that figure, at least. Say it as arithmetic: "it owes $X; with this order $Y; its limit is $Z, so it would need at least $Y".
- **A held order** shows the check that held it and by how much it is over.

## Who Decides

Whoever holds customer credit decides on limits and on releasing a held order; the company's own roster names them, and the answer names them. Sales may ask; the credit owner decides.

## The Line It Doesn't Cross

- **No opinion on any limit.** The answer never says a limit should be raised, lowered, kept or removed, and never says an order should be released or refused. It shows the figures and names who decides.
- **No verdict on a customer.** Paying late is shown in days, not as "unreliable" or "risky".
- **Companies, not individuals.** Credit decisions about sole traders and private persons carry extra rules in many places; this reading is for company customers. Where a customer may be an individual, say so and leave the reading to the credit owner.
- **It doesn't change anything.** Limits, holds and releases are set by a person, in the system that holds the books.
