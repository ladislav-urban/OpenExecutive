# Finding Work Delivered or Done but Not Billed

Revenue leaks quietly. A delivery goes out and nobody raises its invoice. Billable hours are logged and never billed. A project passes a milestone that was due for billing and nothing goes to the customer. Each one is money already earned, and each is usually found late: at the month-end close, when the customer's own audit asks, or never.

Looking for it is routine work, not a judgement call: the records either show an invoice for the work or they don't.

## Where It Hides

- **Deliveries without an invoice.** Goods left the warehouse against a sales order, and the delivery has no sales invoice, or one for fewer items than were delivered. Most systems have a report of delivered items still to be billed. That report is the starting point, not a search through every order.
- **Billable hours not billed.** Time logged against a customer's project as billable, in a period that has ended, with no invoice that covers it. Use the billing summary of timesheets by project or customer, not the timesheets themselves: individual people's hours are a personal record and stay out of the answer.
- **Milestones and schedules passed.** A project billed in stages (a deposit, a milestone, a completion payment) or a contract billed monthly. Compare each stage whose date or condition has passed with the invoices raised against it.
- **Partly billed orders.** An order invoiced for some lines or quantities but not all, or billed at a lower rate than the order says.
- **Recurring billing that stopped.** A monthly service or rental whose invoices stopped while the service went on.

## How to Check

1. **Fix the period.** Usually last month, or everything up to today. Say which.
2. **Deliveries:** list the delivered items still to be billed, with each delivery, its customer, date, items and value.
3. **Hours:** list billable hours not yet invoiced, by project and customer, with the hours, the rate and the value. Report totals per project, never per person.
4. **Schedules:** for each project or contract billed in stages or monthly, list the stages due by now and match each one to an invoice.
5. **Rule out the false alarms.** A delivery invoiced together with a later one, an order agreed to be billed on completion, a free replacement, or hours agreed as non-billable. Say which of these you checked.
6. **Total it.** The value not yet billed, by customer, with the currency and the period.

Every figure comes from the books' own reports, with the company, currency and period. The answer says where each one came from so a person can open it and check.

## Reading It

- **The biggest items first.** One forgotten delivery is often worth more than a month of stray hours.
- **How old each one is.** An invoice raised a week late is a nuisance. One raised three months late is harder to collect, and the customer may dispute it.
- **The pattern.** The same customer, project or kind of work showing up month after month points to a process gap (who raises invoices after a delivery, or when a milestone passes), not a one-off slip.

## Who Acts

Raising an invoice for work already delivered or done is routine, not a decision about money. Name who does it from the people the company has: usually whoever raises sales invoices, or the project manager for a milestone. Where something needs a decision, such as billing a customer for hours the contract may not cover, or how to handle an invoice months late, name who decides: usually whoever owns the customer relationship or the contract.

## What It Is Not

- It is not a revenue estimate. It covers only work the records show as delivered or done.
- It doesn't post anything. Invoices are raised by a person, in the system that holds the books.
